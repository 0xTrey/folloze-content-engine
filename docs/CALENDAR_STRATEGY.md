# Calendar Strategy

## Current launch mode

The first 14 publishing days are manually curated in `content/calendar.yaml`. This sequence is designed to establish category ownership, prove product depth, and create reusable comparison assets early.

## Sequence logic

- Days 1, 6, and 14 are short point-of-view blog posts to sharpen Folloze's narrative.
- Days 2 and 8 define category language Folloze wants to own in AI search and answer engines.
- Days 3, 7, 11, and 13 are operational guides that connect product capabilities to execution.
- Days 5, 9, and 12 address common objections, reporting questions, and governance friction.
- Days 5, 9, and 12 are also the highest-value comparison or buyer-evaluation moments for pipeline influence.

## Future Juno operating model

- Juno owns weekly calendar maintenance after launch.
- Inputs should come from product marketing, field feedback, competitive intelligence, and performance data from published posts.
- Juno should score topics on four dimensions: strategic relevance, search/AEO potential, proof availability, and conversion potential.
- Human guidance should stay focused on campaign priorities, proof-point updates, and taboo claims. Juno should handle sequencing and draft generation.

## Review standard

- Juno can generate and package each post without manual intervention.
- Final approval stays with the content engine operator until the marketing handoff is complete.
- Calendar updates should preserve a mix of category, competitive, proof-led, and operational content.
